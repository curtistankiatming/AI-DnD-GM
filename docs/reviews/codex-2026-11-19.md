## Findings

### 1. The end of the written campaign gives terminal players no onward direction

**Where** `q-aftermath-after`, `patrol`, returning to `town-highpass`.

**What** The result says “This season’s adventure is complete.” The board then says “Nothing posted.” The party was still 1,255 XP short of level 11. The game had marked the books finished and made new adventure requests available, but the terminal did not communicate that state. After I supplied the bundled draft, the printed commission response repeated the previous civic lodging message. The new quest appeared when I opened `town`.

**Why it matters** A player who can see more levels can mistake the intended handoff for missing content or an incomplete quest.

**Suggestion** Show that the written campaign is complete, explain that further expeditions use the AI storyteller, and point to the supported adventure request interface. Print the title and location of a newly commissioned quest immediately. Keep the no-AI endpoint clear so players know where their written campaign ends.

**Severity** Major.

### 2. The Oath still promises a personal moment and delivers a summary

**Where** `q-trial-start` / `investigate`, `q-trial-crisis` / `resolve`, `q-trial-after` / `accept`.

**What** The choice invites me to discuss what the road taught us. The failed check says “You disagree about the past, but the disagreement clarifies the question,” without saying what anybody remembers or disputes. Protecting the gate after the failed parley made the trial's premise work in combat. The aftermath then announces “Your class mastery is learned and ready to use” before `accept`, which is when the skill is actually granted, and “choose your path” leads directly back to town.

**Why it matters** The player has little chance to express what the oath means, and the text makes it unclear when the reward is earned.

**Suggestion** Let a companion raise one choice the player actually made and ask the hero to state a short oath. Use “Swear the Oath of the Road” for `accept` and announce mastery after that decision.

**Severity** Major.

### 3. The best later scenes still lack the personal exchanges that would connect them

**Where** `q-archive-crisis` / `resolve`, `q-signal-after` / `refugees`, `q-beacon-crisis` / `resolve`.

**What** The archivist asked Maren to deliver the letters in the successful peaceful route, then her personal quest resolved with “Watchful: +1 AC” and no delivery scene. At the tower, “Nessa receives her mentor’s last reliable map” leads directly to caravan priorities. At the beacon, “The Single Voice offers perfect safety at the cost of every keeper’s choice” gives the antagonist's position in summary. Corvane and Elske did not speak in the played route.

**Why it matters** The early rewrite shows how much a few lines of dialogue can do for motives and relationships, and the later chapters leave those moments offstage.

**Suggestion** Give Maren one letter and a recipient, Nessa a line with her mentor, and the Single Voice a named speaker who can explain a concrete fear. The bridge camp is a strong model for showing what a mission costs the people involved.

**Severity** Major.

### 4. Fight starts can repeat the same outcome, and Continue can show a town header during combat

**Where** `q-breach-crisis` and `q-beacon-crisis` / `resolve`, `q-aftermath-crisis` / `objective`, and the road's board return from `town-briarwatch`.

**What** The Quiet District's failed parley prints “A few residents wake, enough to begin an evacuation” twice in one result. At the beacon the same happens to “The first voices answer, then falter under the pressure.” The text is preserved, an improvement over its earlier omission. On returning to the road fight, the terminal still headed the screen “Briarwatch · The Bell & Bramble” above the live wagon encounter and lock objective. The retry completed normally.

**Why it matters** A duplicated outcome obscures the sequence leading to a fight, and the wrong location heading makes it less obvious that Continue worked.

**Suggestion** Render the choice result once, then show the encounter opening and turns in their own block. Use the encounter location as the header when a town action restarts a fight.

**Severity** Minor.

### 5. The relief convoy advertises options the player cannot have yet

**Where** `q-aftermath-crisis`, `objective`.

**What** The description advertises “objective-focused alternatives for Saboteurs and Lorekeepers.” Rowan had Battle Surge available, and it helped the next attack. It did not replace a gate check. A fresh character is offered this expedition at level 10, and those branches open at level 20. The generic approach screen also repeats “The route ahead offers a careful approach or a direct crossing,” after the player has selected the route.

**Why it matters** The first expedition that showcases mastery should prepare the player to use the ability they just earned, and its identical approach screen gets in the way of that setup.

**Suggestion** Describe the character's actual mastery and explain how it can help while the convoy waits. Replace the generic approach paragraph with something specific to the gallery, the convoy, and the gate.

**Severity** Minor.

## Three things to keep

1. **The rescue restart makes preparation matter.** Resetting the unfinished lock and retaining the favourable approach made retreat a recoverable setback with a real cost.
2. **The bridge camp uses humour to reveal character.** Orin's “It's true enough for tonight” after Nessa challenges Rowan's reassurance, followed by his eating Maren's stew, does more for his relationship with the group than the approval number does.
3. **Protecting people can win an encounter.** Saving the trial gate, getting the convoy through, and closing the sluice moved the story while enemies remained. Battle Surge fit alongside those objectives without replacing the player's decision about when to fight and when to work the gate.
