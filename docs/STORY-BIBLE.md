# Briarwatch story bible (free storyline, levels 1 to 20)

The written campaign that plays with no AI. Approved by Curtis on 12 November
2026, revised after a Fable editorial review the same day. The editable master
copy is the Claude Doc "Briarwatch story bible"; this file is the copy writers
and reviewing agents use inside the repository. Keep the two in step.

The free storyline is a two-book campaign that ends at level 20, where the hero
chooses their 2nd job. Book One is the original story (the bell, then the
Single Voice), rewritten deeper and warmer. Book Two is new: ten chapters for
levels 11 to 20.

## Tone

Grounded high fantasy about ordinary people deciding who gets to protect them.
The stakes are real and the villains have reasons. Humour comes from the people,
never from the narrator winking at the player.

**Story first.** Every scene has to move something: a clue, a relationship, a
choice with a cost. A funny scene still has to do one of those jobs.

**Where comedy comes from:**

- **Character habits under pressure.** Orin cannot bluff, so any plan that needs
  him to lie goes slightly wrong in a way he has to live with.
- **People being people.** A council that argues about chairs while the world
  ends. A merchant who invoices you for the bridge you saved.
- **The gap between heroics and logistics.** Somebody has to carry the bell.
  Somebody has to feed the refugees. Somebody forgot the rope.
- **Running threads, not running gags.** A joke returns only if it has changed.
  Maren's terrible stew is funny once, and means something else the night before
  the final battle, when Orin quietly asks for a bowl.

**What we avoid:**

- One-line quips after every fight, or banter that ignores someone dying.
- Modern slang, pop-culture nods or fourth-wall jokes.
- Making a character stupid so a joke works. They can be wrong, proud, tired or
  stubborn, never dumb.
- Laughing at the helpless. Comedy aims at the powerful, the pompous and the
  heroes themselves.
- The narrator explaining a joke or a feeling ("which for Maren is a declaration
  of love", "nobody laughs").
- Rules language inside story text ("this does not grant a reward").

**No quota.** A lighter moment goes where the people in the scene would actually
have one, and nowhere else. The darkest chapters (the drowned archive, the quiet
district, Fenmere under the Gloam, Hollin's Reach) have none until the aftermath.

Orin and Maren are old acquaintances who bicker like siblings about risk. Maren
mothers Nessa, and Nessa resents it loudly while quietly relying on it. Orin and
Nessa start as opposites (wait versus go) and end as the pair who trust each
other most in a fight. Approval of the hero unlocks extra conversation scenes.

**Can companions leave?** Not for good mid-story. If approval falls too low, a
companion walks out for one chapter and can be won back. A permanent parting is
possible only in the epilogue.

**The hero** is a newcomer Briarwatch took in. What they want is up to the
player, but the story keeps asking: who are you doing this for? Their answer
becomes the Second Oath at level 20.

## The story, levels 1 to 20

One question runs through both books: **who gets to decide what keeps people
safe?** Book One answers "not one person alone". Book Two asks: if many voices
must decide together, how do they agree before the house burns down?

**What the wards are for.** Wards ring back **the Gloam**, a cold living mist
from the old places that takes the warmth and memory out of whatever it touches,
and drives fire and flood ahead of it. That is why a silent bell is terrifying.

### Book One: The Bell Beneath Briarwatch (levels 1 to 10, rewritten)

Same plot, choices and rewards, so existing saves still work.

- **Act I (Briarwatch, the bell):** the village feels like home before it is
  threatened. Aveline, Dera and Tovin get proper conversations. Nessa's rescue is
  a real scene where she argues with Orin while hanging upside down.
- **Acts II to IV (Briarwood, hollow, vault):** companions argue the plan in the
  antechamber as a real conversation. Sable makes his case before the fight. His
  fate is always stated.
- **Expeditions (levels 2 to 10):** each gains a talk scene with the people
  involved and a camp scene where companions react. The bridge, archive and
  signal tower carry each companion's personal story. "After" choices get
  visible costs.
- **The enemy gets a face:** High Warden Corvane of Central Command speaks
  through the Single Voice at the beacon. Its design was Elske's.
- **Clean-up:** machine words (pumping station, relays, command engine) become
  in-world ones (sluice-house, echo-stones, the iron chorus).
- **Ending at level 10:** the hero takes the Oath of the Road; the towns must
  form the council the old wardens designed, and nobody agrees on anything.

### Book Two: The Council of Small Voices (levels 11 to 20, new)

New places: **Merrowgate**, a harbour city run by guilds (levels 13 to 16), and
**Cinder Fold**, the mining town over the forge where the first bells were cast
(levels 16 to 20). Briarwatch is visited at levels 12 and 17.

**Elske versus Sable.** Sable took power in secret; Elske asks for it in public.
Her offer: **one named Warden of the Chorus, chosen by the council, with
emergency command of every ward for one year, recallable by a two-thirds
vote.** It echoes the player's own choice after the quiet district.

**Carried forward from Book One** (old-save default in brackets): Sable's fate
(imprisoned at Highpass); whether Lysa was spared (spared); the level 8 choice
of independent wards or a charter (independent) and the quiet district choice
(oversight council), which set the council's mood; each town's "after" decision
(neutral); companion approval (as saved).

**Side stories:** two short jobs per hub, grounded in the main story, and one
companion camp scene per level.

**XP, levels 11 to 20:** a chapter pays about 30% of a level, a side job about
12%, battles the usual share by difficulty. No XP is inflated.

## How scenes are written

Conversation scenes live in `src/data/scenes.json`; see its `_note` and
`tests/scenes.test.js`. Lines are `{"say": "<companion id or a name>", "text": "..."}`
or `{"narrate": "..."}`. Choices change companion approval only, never XP or
loot. The first example is the camp scene after the bridge (`bridge-camp`).
